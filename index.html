Formation Systems

Infrastructure as a living system

Formation Systems models infrastructure as a living system: the networks of people, practices and institutions that a community depends on and must keep renewing in order to last. The model brings together three fields: biological autopoiesis, how living systems continually produce themselves; organizational cybernetics, how organizations coordinate and remain viable; and social neuroscience, how people attune to one another through shared rhythm and attention.

The research topic is Christian discipleship. A church forms its members through rites, roles, groups and a shared calendar, and those relationships must be continually remade as members are born, join, leave and die.

Discipleship as infrastructure

Two models ask what changes when discipleship is understood not as knowledge transfer, but as continuous participation in an operationally closed, self-producing environment. Discipleship becomes the infrastructure, modeled autopoietically: a body whose relationships keep producing the very components through which its members are formed.

The Computational Model

Catholic and Protestant discipleship infrastructures are complex adaptive systems, simulated here as two versions of one computational model. Each is a stochastic agent-based model: members move through stages of belonging, and their bonds to the community decay over time and are renewed through sacraments, groups and the liturgical calendar. These relationships form a weighted, directed network of members, groups, roles and sacraments, calibrated to available datasets and updated day by day as the simulation runs. At the scale of the whole Church, populations on each continent evolve through linked difference equations. Each model element is listed in each model’s paper with its status as data or hypothesis, along with its sources.
