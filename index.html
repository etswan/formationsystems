What happened

    Components

    • Sacraments: produce and renew relations
    • Roles: pastor, deacon, catechists, musicians, directors
    • Groups: family faith formation, OCIA, choir, young adults, Bible study, service
    • Religious orders: a local community, and orders linking parishes
    • Liturgical calendar: the ring that keeps every church on the same days
    • Members, with catechumens and inquirers outside
    • Christ: the light at the centre, not one of the components
    How to read it

    Strike the body and members scatter, then their relations draw them back. That is homeostasis: the form springs back.

    Leave it running for a year and the parish remakes itself. Members leave, and new ones come through infant baptism, OCIA and the Easter Vigil. Links fade unless the Eucharist and group life renew them. The members change while the organisation persists: that is autopoiesis.

    Christ is the light at the centre. Every relation is lit by it, but it is not a component the system produces, which is where the autopoietic description reaches its limit.

    Try Closed leadership. Leaders stop renewing their ties to groups, groups fall dormant, members held by one group drift out, and the boundary hardens. Then drop the shared calendar and switch to all churches: Sundays stop lighting up together.

    Remove a group to test regeneration. Losing the choir changes the parish’s structure, but members find other groups and the parish stays itself. Losing OCIA or family faith formation removes the processes that produce new members, so the parish slowly cannot regenerate its own components.
