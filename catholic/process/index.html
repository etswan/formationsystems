The Parish as Process

The Body of Christ Model through a processual and performative lens

This is the same parish as on the autopoietic page, with the same people and the same calibrated rates of baptism, reception, practice, leaving and death. What changes is how its life is organized. There are no fixed groups to which people are assigned. People gather, and where they keep gathering a group takes shape; groups grow, fade, dissolve and form again, and new ones arise from the overlap of old ones. Practice is a degree of participation that drifts, not a switch. Each person and each gathering carries a yearly rhythm that can fall into or out of step with the liturgical year.

The parish is never fully determined by its structures. They condition what can happen next, and two runs of the same parish can go different ways.

The Parish as Process

Processual lens · Version 1.2 · each dot is one person · Model developed by Emma T. Swan

Quiet chant sounds the parish's common life, fuller when more people come to Mass and at the great feasts. Each gathering that meets adds its own voice, louder the more people it holds and carry it, so the sound grows richer as more gatherings meet. Voices drift out of tune and out of time when the parish loses its shared rhythm. Bells answer enthusiasm, and a high, bright peal greets a new gathering arising from others.

    What has happened

      Reading the parish
      • Each dot is a person, colored by age: children, youth, young adults, adults and older members. The brighter and larger, the more they take part.
      • Gold is enthusiasm after an event or a stroke.
      • Rings are gatherings, sized by membership; their pulse is the gathering's rhythm. Dashed rings could form.
      • The ring around the middle is the liturgical year, colored by season, with today marked. People pulse with their own yearly rhythm; those in step with the Church's year pulse together.

      How this lens works

      The autopoietic page asks whether a parish produces the processes that produce it and keeps a boundary. This page asks what the parish's present makes possible next. It keeps everything that was measured and calibrated on the autopoietic page and changes the organization that was assumed.

      What stays the same

      Every person is simulated, week by week. Births and baptisms, reception at the Easter Vigil, slipping and returning, leaving, moving and death follow the autopoietic page's calibrated rules and rates. Run without perturbations, the parish keeps weekly attendance near 25%, regular practice among about 30% of cradle Catholics and a third or more of converts, about five infant baptisms and 0.7 adults received a year in a parish of 600, and about 1.1% of members leaving each year, close to the autopoietic page. Bonds run about 5% higher and group membership about three points higher, mostly because new gatherings arise and draw people in. The share of members of childbearing age assumed in the baptism rate was recalibrated from 0.23 to 0.22 so that infant baptisms meet their target. calibrated

      What changes

      • Gatherings instead of fixed groups. People are drawn to gatherings at the same rates as before, but a gathering exists only while people keep coming. It is carried from within by its committed members; with too few of them it fades, after three years dormant or half a year with fewer than three people it dissolves, and a kind of gathering that has gone can form again among people who want it. hypothesis
      • The adjacent possible. When committed members come to share two gatherings, a new one can arise from them, such as Word and works from Bible study and service, and draws people from both. Which ones arise, and when, depends on the parish's own history. hypothesis
      • Continuous practice. Regular and occasional practice are tendencies, not states: participation moves gradually toward one or the other over about two months, and changes at the calibrated rates. partly grounded
      • Rhythm. Each person and each gathering has a yearly cycle, pulled toward the liturgical year by the shared calendar and Sunday practice, and toward one another through the gatherings people share. When the rhythm breaks, renewal at Mass, feasts and meetings weakens a little. hypothesis
      • Enthusiasm by touch. Events land where you tap and spread from there; a stroke across people lends them enthusiasm in proportion to its speed. Disruptions are kept in the panel. hypothesis

      What it does not claim

      This page is a program, so it cannot be indeterminate in Kauffman's sense: every state it reaches follows from its rules and its random numbers. What changes is the stance. Nothing about which gatherings exist, who carries them or which new ones arise is listed in advance; those patterns emerge from how the parish and the visitor move through a field of conditions. The five states in the panel are a reading of the simulated parish, not a measurement of any real one.
